## Nuance by claim
1. Johnston first zig-zagged making smoke and opened fire at 0710, then turned in. Sprague did order smoke (0700, HO) and torpedo attacks (0716 and 0742 per SA; 0735 per HO). Write "without waiting for orders", never "Sprague never ordered it."
2. Use "Center Force", not "Central Force".
3. "Couldn't outrun them" is fair. The real speed threat was the 35-knot cruisers and destroyers.
4. DANFS gives no launch time. Launch was about 0720; the hit came at 0724–0727.
5. NHHC consistently credits Johnston; I found no NHHC dispute. "A torpedo wrecked her bow" is the safest wording. Suzuya had already been slowed by aircraft (CFS, about 0735) and sank at 1322 that day. **Don't use NH 82400**: that missing bow is from a submarine torpedo on 6 Nov 1944 (CFK).
6. NHHC sources disagree on the 0730 hits. DJ says "three 18.1-inch shells from Yamato… in addition to three 6.1-inch". SA says "6- and 14-inch". Write "heavy battleship shells, probably Yamato's."
7. Two separate funnels is the wrong ship. Chōkai and Haguro had two (standard reference, not fetched). Kumano and Suzuya had five twin turrets, inferred to be three forward and two aft.
8. Share the credit with aircraft. The attempt to shield Gambier Bay failed.
12. Say "a little after 9 a.m." The script's "smoke, repeated attacks, confusion" line is fair if aircraft are included.
13. Samuel B. Roberts' sinking time (about 1005) comes only from secondary sources. Johnston's rated complement was 273; 327 were aboard.
14. Don't say Evans "went down with his ship". Quote the MOH page, not H-038-2, which drops "powerful". Keep "in the U.S. Navy".
15. The quote is a 1945 recollection with no transcript, and DJ's commissioning account omits it. Avoid longer "never retreat" versions.
16. 80-G photos are official USN images and public domain. Shepler's NH 79033-KN is a painting. I found no image of Kalinin Bay being straddled. The smoke in 80-G-287439 is only "posited" to be Johnston's.
17. It was a morning battle, so no sunset. By my calculation the sun rose in the ESE (azimuth about 102°), behind Taffy 3. The Japanese came from the NNW.
18. The Japanese officer is unnamed in the primary sources. Wikipedia's Yukikaze/Terauchi detail is flagged as possibly self-published.

## Notes for script writer
- "Without waiting for orders, Evans made smoke and charged." Then "battleship shells, likely Yamato's", not "three 14-inch".
- Kumano: "a torpedo wrecked her bow; she limped out". She was not sunk at Samar.
- Cruiser visuals: one big raked funnel and five twin turrets.
- Kurita turned away "a little after 9". Credit aircraft too.
- Evans was "last seen in the water". Commissioning words and the salute: "as survivors remembered".
- Visuals: grey, squally dawn with red, pink and green-yellow splashes. No golden backlit sun behind the Japanese. Unarmed dummy runs are fine to show.
